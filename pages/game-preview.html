Warriors at Clippers preview: what has to change after Sunday.

The cleanest way to frame this matchup is not to treat it like a fresh series opener. It is an elimination rematch built almost directly on top of the Clippers' 115-110 win on April 12. That result locked Los Angeles into the No. 9 seed and dropped Golden State into this road Play-In spot.

Quick map
  • Why the rematch matters
  • What favors the Clippers
  • What favors the Warriors
  • Three things to watch

Why the rematch matters

Most pregame pages spend time on broad season context. That still matters here, but the more useful starting point is the immediate evidence from Sunday night. The Clippers got enough scoring from the second unit, finished better from deep in the fourth quarter, and kept the Warriors from flipping the game after a tight third.

That makes Wednesday less about discovery and more about adjustment. Golden State has recent tape from the exact same floor. The Clippers have the confidence of already winning the setup game that created this bracket slot.

What favors the Clippers

The biggest confirmed edge from the most recent meeting was bench production. Los Angeles won the reserve scoring line 71-56, and that gap was not empty noise. Bennedict Mathurin put up 20 points, nine rebounds and eight assists off the bench, while Bogdan Bogdanovic added 17 points and hit five threes.

That matters in a Play-In setting because second-unit stability becomes part of survival. If the game turns into a short-rotation, pressure-possession contest, the Clippers at least have recent proof that their supporting scoring can keep the offense moving.

What favors the Warriors

Stephen Curry still gave Golden State a usable offensive foundation in the latest matchup, scoring 24 points in 29 minutes. The Warriors also kept the April 12 game close enough that one cleaner closing stretch could have changed the result.

That is the reason this is still a live matchup instead of a one-sided setup. Golden State does not need to reinvent itself in 48 hours. It needs a sharper fourth quarter, cleaner bench support, and better control of the possessions that drifted away late in the last meeting.

Three things to watch

Bench scoring again

If the Clippers' second unit creates the same scoring gap, the math gets hard for Golden State.

Late threes

The Clippers hit five threes in the fourth quarter on Sunday. If that repeats, the game can tilt quickly.

Curry's support

The Warriors need enough offense around Curry to keep the pressure from narrowing onto one source.

My closing read

The most accurate preview line is simple: the Clippers have the recent win, the home floor, and the cleaner proof point from Sunday. The Warriors still have enough shotmaking and experience to turn the game if the closing stretch looks different. That makes this a strong Play-In site to build before tipoff because the intent is clear: time, stakes, and the fast rematch context all matter.
